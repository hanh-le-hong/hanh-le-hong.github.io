In internet culture, there is a simple rule called the **90-9-1 principle**: out of 100 people, 90 consume, 9 distribute or curate, and only 1 actually builds something new.

For most of my life, I lived comfortably between the 90 and the 9. I read articles, watched YouTube lectures, analyzed products, and distributed ideas through marketing campaigns. Because I came from a non-tech, no-code background, building software always felt like a locked room that belonged to engineers.

## What Changed

AI opened that locked door for me.

For the first time, I realized I could describe a problem clearly, break it down into logical steps, and work alongside AI coding agents to turn an idea into a working tool.

I am still a complete newbie on this path. My projects are tiny:

- **Snapcard**: A small web app to send digital postcards with a single link.
- **Marketing Systems Lab**: A public notebook on GitHub where I practice measurement, funnels, and AI workflows.
- **AiPRIL**: This personal website and its own lightweight Markdown CMS.

## The Goal Is Not to Oversell

I don't want to pretend to be a hardcore software engineer. By day, I am a 9-to-5 marketer and operator who puts business impact first.

By night and on weekends, I just want to be part of the **1%** who builds, even if what I build starts small.
